Buying guide: how to choose

What a heated airer actually costs to run

Most heated airers have no thermostat: they draw close to their rated power the whole time they are on. A 230 W airer costs €0.087 an hour at €0.38/kWh, so a six-hour drying session is about €0.52. A 1,000 W heated dryer with a cover dries faster but costs €0.380 an hour — over four times as much.

Heated airer, dehumidifier or tumble dryer?

A heated airer adds warmth to the clothes but the water still goes into the room air, which is a problem in a damp Irish home. Pairing an airer with a dehumidifier in the same closed room is the fastest non-tumble option and removes the moisture instead of spreading it. A tumble dryer is fastest of all but is the most expensive to run per load.

Covers speed things up

A cover traps warm air around the clothes, which can noticeably cut drying time. Several airers sell one as an extra; check whether it is included before comparing prices.

Capacity and space

Three-tier airers hold the most laundry in the smallest floor area. Winged, single-level airers are easier to load and fold flatter for storage. Check the maximum load and the folded size against where you will actually keep it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a heated clothes airer cost to run in Ireland?

A typical 220–230 W heated airer costs about €0.084–€0.087 an hour at €0.38/kWh. Most have no thermostat and draw near their rated power the whole time, so a six-hour load is roughly €0.50. On a night rate of €0.18/kWh the same load is about €0.25.

Is a heated airer cheaper than a tumble dryer?

Per hour, yes by a wide margin: a heated airer draws around 220–230 W while a tumble dryer typically draws 2,000 W or more when heating. The airer is slower, so compare per load, not per hour — but for most households a heated airer is still much cheaper per load.

Do heated airers cause damp and mould?

The water in your clothes has to go somewhere, and with an airer it goes into the room. In a small closed room that raises humidity and condensation. Running a dehumidifier in the same room, or drying near an open window or extractor, prevents it.

Can I leave a heated airer on overnight?

Many people do, and running it overnight on a night-rate or smart-meter tariff roughly halves the cost. Follow the manufacturer's instructions, keep it away from curtains and bedding, and don't cover it with anything other than the cover designed for it.
