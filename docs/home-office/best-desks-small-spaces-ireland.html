Buying guide: how to choose

How do you measure a small room correctly before buying a desk?

Measure the actual clear wall space, not just the room dimensions, and separately measure the depth needed for the chair to pull out fully — a desk that fits the wall but leaves no room to sit down properly isn't a workable setup. Under-110cm-wide desks are the common threshold for Irish box rooms and apartment corners. A box room or bedroom corner rarely has a clean rectangle to work with: a radiator, a window sill, a sloped ceiling under the stairs, or a door swing all eat into usable floor area before the desk arrives. Width alone doesn't capture the problem either — a narrow desk that's too deep for the alcove, or a corner desk that clashes with a door swing, fails just as often as one that's simply too big. Sketch the room with radiator, door swing and window included before shortlisting desks.

Foldable and wall-mounted desks: the space-vs-stability trade-off

Foldable desks that fix to the wall and drop flat when not in use reclaim the most floor space of any option, which matters in a room doing double duty as a spare bedroom or storage. The trade-off is stability: wall-folding desks rely on the wall fixing and a support leg or bracket, and most have lower weight capacity and more flex under typing pressure than a standard four-leg desk of the same size. Wall-mounted fixed desks (no fold) split the difference - sturdier than folding models since there's no hinge to add flex, but they permanently claim the wall space even when not in use. For daily use with a monitor and keyboard, a fixed small desk generally out-stabilises a folding one at a similar price; folding suits occasional or laptop-only use.

Storage that doesn't eat floor space

In a small room, storage built into the desk - drawers, a shelf riser, an under-desk cable tray doubling as a shelf - saves buying separate furniture that would otherwise eat more floor space than the desk itself. Vertical storage above the desk (a wall shelf or pegboard) keeps the floor footprint unchanged while adding capacity, which matters more in a box room than a filing cabinet ever will. Before adding drawers, check they don't reduce usable knee clearance, since a desk with a deep drawer unit on one side can make a compact desk feel narrower than its stated width once seated. For genuinely tight rooms, a desk with no built-in storage but a slightly smaller footprint often works better than a "space-saving" desk loaded with drawers that eat the same space they claim to save.

How do you manage cables on a desk in a tiny room?

Cable clutter is more visible and more disruptive in a small room than a large one, since there's nowhere for a tangle of cables to hide out of sight. A basic cable tray or clip-on channel under the desktop keeps power and monitor cables off the floor, which also matters practically in a box room doubling as a walkway or with limited floor space around the desk legs. Desks with a built-in grommet hole for cables to drop straight down are worth prioritising over a flat, hole-free desktop, since retrofitting a grommet is awkward on most desktop materials. In a shared or multi-use room, a magnetic or velcro cable organiser that fully detaches the setup for evenings and weekends is worth the small extra cost. None of this needs to be expensive - a €10 clip-on tray solves most of the visual clutter that makes a small room feel more cramped than it is.

Will a desk fit through an Irish terraced house doorway?

Older Irish terraced houses commonly have narrow hallways, tight stair turns, and internal doorways around 75-80cm wide, all of which can turn a flat-packed desk delivery into a genuine access problem, particularly for one-piece desktops over 120cm. Check the desk's largest single panel dimension against your narrowest doorway and stairwell turn before ordering, not just the room it's going into - a desk that fits the box room easily can still fail to get there. Most small-space desks ship flat-packed specifically because of this constraint, with the largest panel being the desktop itself; genuinely oversized one-piece desks are rare for this reason. For apartment blocks, also check lift dimensions if the unit is above ground floor. Asking the retailer for exact packed dimensions before ordering, rather than relying on the assembled desk's footprint, is the single easiest way to avoid a failed delivery.

Frequently asked questions

What's a realistic minimum desk width for someone who needs a monitor and keyboard?

For a single monitor and keyboard with enough surface for a mouse and notepad, 100-110cm width is the practical minimum most people find comfortable; below that, monitor and keyboard start competing for space and the mouse ends up working in a cramped strip. Depth matters as much as width in a small room - 50-60cm is enough for a monitor at proper viewing distance, though a monitor arm frees up desk depth by lifting the screen off the surface entirely, which is often the more effective fix than hunting for a wider desk in a tight room.

Are corner desks a good use of space in a box room?

Corner desks maximise usable surface area for the floor space they occupy, since they use a room's corner - often otherwise wasted space - rather than a flat wall run. They work best in a genuinely square or rectangular box room with a clear corner; in an oddly shaped attic conversion or a room with a radiator or window in the corner, they can be harder to fit than a straight desk. The trade-off is chair clearance: corner desks need enough pulled-back space for the chair to swing, which a straight wall desk in a narrow room sometimes handles better.

Can a small desk still support a standing desk converter?

Yes, as long as the desk surface is stable and deep enough to hold the converter's footprint plus some working space in front of it - most converters need roughly 60cm of desk depth minimum. What matters more than the desk's brand or price is that it doesn't flex noticeably under the converter's weight when raised, since a converter concentrates significant weight (monitor, keyboard tray, its own frame) onto a smaller area than a full desktop would. Thin veneer or particleboard desks under about 18mm thickness are the most likely to flex; solid or thicker engineered wood tops handle a converter with less concern.

How do I stop a small desk feeling cramped with a laptop and monitor both?

A monitor arm is the single biggest space-saver here, since it lifts the screen off the desktop entirely and frees that depth for a laptop, keyboard or notepad underneath or beside it - far more effective than buying a wider desk to fit both flat. Positioning the laptop in a stand next to or below the monitor, rather than flat on the desk in front of it, also reclaims meaningful surface area. For genuinely tight setups, a keyboard tray that slides under the desktop when not typing adds working depth back for other tasks during the day.

Is it worth building a desk into an alcove instead of buying freestanding?

A built-in alcove desk (custom-cut to fit a chimney breast recess or stair understair space) uses awkward space that most freestanding desks can't fit into at all, and avoids wasted gaps at the sides that a standard-width desk leaves in a non-standard alcove. It costs more and takes longer to install than buying off the shelf, and it's not portable if you move house or rearrange the room later. For a genuinely irregular space - sloped ceiling, chimney breast, stair understair nook - it's often the only option that doesn't waste space; for a standard rectangular box room, freestanding is simpler and cheaper.

What size desk fits a typical Irish box room?

Irish box rooms - the standard small third bedroom in a typical semi-detached or terraced house - commonly measure around 2m x 2.5m once a bed or wardrobe is removed, which comfortably fits a desk up to about 120cm wide against one wall with room to walk past. Apartment corners set up as a workspace are usually tighter again, often only fitting an 80-100cm desk. Measuring the specific wall run available, rather than assuming a standard box room size, is the safer approach, since radiators, sloped ceilings and door swings vary the usable space considerably between houses of the same nominal room size.
