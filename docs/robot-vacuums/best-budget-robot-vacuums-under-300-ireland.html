Buying guide: how to choose

What kind of navigation do you actually get under €300 now?

A few years ago, sub-€300 meant random-bounce navigation with no memory between cleans; that's no longer true across the board. Several budget-tier robots now ship with genuine lidar or basic vision mapping, giving room-by-room cleaning, resumable cleans after charging, and app-drawn no-go zones — features that were mid-range exclusives not long ago. What you still don't get at this price is the mapping precision or multi-floor memory of pricier models: budget lidar units typically hold one or two saved maps rather than several, and re-mapping after furniture moves takes longer. For a typical Irish terraced house or apartment with a fairly stable layout, that's rarely a practical problem. It becomes one if you rearrange furniture often or want separate saved maps for upstairs and downstairs — check the spec sheet's map-storage number specifically, not just whether it says smart navigation.

Mopping at this price is wiping, not washing

Nearly every budget robot vacuum under €300 that includes mopping does it with a static or vibrating pad dragged behind the unit, not a rotating, pressurised pad — the mechanism reserved for mid-range and premium models. That means it wipes rather than scrubs, fine for daily maintenance on tile or sealed wood to catch dust and light spills, but it won't shift dried-on marks or grease the way a proper mop would. Water flow is usually gravity-fed and can't be finely adjusted, so pad dampness varies as the tank empties across a clean. None of this is a defect at the price; it's simply what mopping means at this tier, and it's worth treating it as a bonus feature on top of vacuuming rather than a real substitute for occasional manual mopping in an Irish kitchen that sees muddy shoes and dog paws most of the year.

Obstacle avoidance: the real gap versus mid-range

The clearest gap between budget and mid-range robots is obstacle handling, not suction. Budget units at this price mostly rely on basic infrared or bump sensors, which catch furniture legs and walls reliably but frequently miss low, soft, or dark obstacles — phone chargers, shoes left by the door, dark socks on a dark carpet, pet mess. Mid-range and premium models increasingly use small cameras with object recognition to identify and route around these specifically. For a genuinely tidy home with hard floors, this gap barely matters. For a home with kids, a dog, or a habit of dropping cables on the floor, it means more pre-clean tidying to avoid a tangled brush or a smeared mess, and it's the single biggest quality-of-life difference buyers moving up from this price bracket report first when they upgrade.

Budget self-empty docks: convenience with a catch

A handful of budget robots now include self-empty docks, extending manual bin-emptying from every clean to roughly every three to four weeks. It sounds like a mid-range feature at a budget price, and largely is one, but the dust bags themselves are a recurring cost worth budgeting for separately — typically sold in packs, running out to somewhere in the region of €15-20 for a few months' supply depending on how often the robot runs. The docks are also physically larger than a basic charging dock, which matters more than it sounds in a small Dublin terraced house or apartment where hallway or utility-room floor space is already tight. Measure the dock's footprint against your actual available floor space before assuming a self-empty budget model will fit where the old charging-only dock sat.

Buying secondhand or outlet stock to stretch the budget further

Outlet, refurbished and previous-generation stock is one of the most reliable ways to get mid-range navigation and build quality for a genuinely budget price, provided the seller is clear about warranty terms. A previous year's mid-range model, discounted once its successor launches, frequently outperforms a current-year budget model on mapping precision and obstacle handling for similar or less money. The catch is warranty length and support — refurbished units often carry six or twelve months rather than the standard two years, and it's worth confirming whether that cover comes from an Irish or EU-based seller rather than a cross-border marketplace listing, given the parts-availability friction that's become more common since Brexit. Buying refurbished from an Irish retailer's own outlet section is generally safer than a third-party marketplace listing with vague provenance.

Frequently asked questions

Is a €150 robot vacuum worth buying, or should I save for €300?

Below roughly €150, most models still use bump navigation without any mapping memory, meaning every clean starts from scratch with overlapping, inefficient passes and no no-go zones. That's a real functional gap, not just a missing nice-to-have — it means longer clean times, more missed spots, and no way to keep the robot out of a specific room. Between €150 and €300, lidar and basic mapping become common, which is where most of the practical usability jump happens. If the budget stretches to €250-300, it's generally worth it; below €150, you're often better off saving a bit longer or considering outlet stock on a discontinued mid-range model instead.

Do budget robot vacuums handle multiple rooms well?

The mapping-equipped budget models handle multiple rooms reasonably well now, saving a room-by-room layout and letting you clean the kitchen only, say, rather than the whole floor. The gap shows up in map storage limits — most budget lidar units hold one or two maps rather than several, so a house with a complicated, irregular layout gets less benefit than a simple rectangular layout. Doorways with thick thresholds or steps between rooms, common in older Irish houses, can also confuse budget navigation more than premium sensors, occasionally leaving a room only partially covered.

Are budget robot vacuum batteries good enough for a full house?

Manufacturer figures for sub-€300 models typically list 120-180 minutes of runtime, generally enough for a single-floor clean of an average Irish semi-detached or terraced house on one charge. Larger or more cluttered floor plans may need the auto-resume-after-charging feature to finish, which almost all current budget lidar models include. Battery degradation over two to three years of daily use is the more relevant long-term question — expect a noticeable runtime drop by year two on a budget cell, which is one area where the cost saving versus mid-range battery quality shows up most clearly.

What's the most common complaint with budget robot vacuums in Ireland?

Beyond navigation, the recurring theme in owner reports is app reliability and Wi-Fi reconnection, particularly in older houses with solid or stone walls that weaken signal between rooms. The second most common issue is brush tangling on rugs with longer fringe or loose threads, since budget brush designs are less tangle-resistant than mid-range comb designs. Neither is usually a fault with the specific unit — they're consistent enough across budget-tier brands to be a category trait rather than a bad batch, and worth expecting rather than being surprised by after purchase.

Can I add a self-empty dock later to a budget robot that doesn't have one?

No — self-empty docks are matched to a specific robot model's bin design and charging contacts, so there's no universal add-on that retrofits the feature onto a robot that shipped without it. If automated bin-emptying matters, it needs to be decided at purchase, either by choosing one of the budget models that now include a self-empty dock as standard, or accepting manual emptying and factoring that into which model you buy. Some brands sell the dock separately for the exact same model bought without one, but only within that same product line, never across brands or unrelated models.
