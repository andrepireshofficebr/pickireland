Buying guide: how to choose

What does ‘six to eight weeks unattended’ actually require from a robot vacuum?

The weeks-of-unattended-operation figure premium brands advertise combines three systems working together: a large-capacity base bin, a water and detergent reservoir sized for repeated mop washes, and a dust bag or bin large enough to hold weeks of debris. A heavy-shedding pet or a large open-plan floor plan fills the bin faster than the manufacturer's baseline testing scenario, which assumes a moderate, average household. Reading the spec sheet's stated bin capacity in litres, and comparing it honestly against your own floor size and pet situation, gives a more realistic estimate of your actual emptying interval than trusting the headline number on the box, which is best case rather than typical case.

How good is obstacle AI on premium robot vacuums, really?

Camera-based obstacle recognition on premium models genuinely does what mid-range infrared sensors can't — identifying and routing around cables, shoes, pet mess and low furniture legs by shape rather than just detecting that an object is present. Manufacturer figures on recognition accuracy have climbed significantly over recent generations and owner reports consistently mention markedly fewer stuck-robot incidents than budget or mid-range models produce. The limits worth knowing: recognition confidence typically drops in low light, since most systems rely partly on the camera rather than purely on infrared or lidar depth-sensing, and very low, flat objects — a phone charger cable lying flat, a thin sock — remain harder to reliably distinguish from floor texture than upright obstacles. It's a substantial real improvement over lower tiers, not a guarantee of zero incidents, and worth treating that way when deciding how much pre-clean tidying is still needed.

Does multi-floor mapping solve the stairs problem?

Premium models generally store multiple floor maps and switch between them automatically when manually carried to a different level, a genuine practical advantage for a two- or three-storey Irish house over budget models limited to one saved map. What this doesn't solve is the fundamental stairs problem every robot vacuum shares: none climb them, so multi-floor map memory helps once you've carried the unit upstairs, but it doesn't carry the unit there itself. For a two-storey semi-detached house, this is a manageable inconvenience — carry it up occasionally, or accept ground-floor-only coverage most of the time. For a three-storey townhouse, common in parts of Dublin, it means either genuinely carrying a several-kilogram unit up two flights regularly, or buying a second unit for the top floor, both worth budgeting for honestly before assuming premium multi-floor mapping alone solves a three-storey layout.

What do premium robot vacuum consumables cost a year?

Premium tier consumables run higher than mid-range across every category — larger and more complex main brushes, HEPA filters rated for finer particles, rotating mop pads that wear faster under pressure than static ones, dock dust bags sized for the larger bin, and detergent for self-washing docks. Combined, manufacturer part pricing and independent cost breakdowns put a realistic annual figure noticeably above the roughly €80-150 range typical of simpler models, particularly in the first year when a full set of consumables may need replacing close together. Against that, the automation genuinely reduces time spent on maintenance tasks, which is the actual trade being made — premium tier costs more in parts to save more in manual labour, not to clean meaningfully better than a well-specified mid-range unit on the same floor.

Who should not buy a premium robot vacuum?

This tier is a poor fit for a heavily stepped three-storey house where a single unit can only cover the floor it's parked on regardless of how advanced its dock is — the premium features solve bin-emptying and mop-washing, not stairs, and a household in that situation is often better served by two simpler mid-range units, one per main floor, than one premium unit carried between three levels. It's also poor value for a small, tidy, mostly-hard-floor apartment or terraced house with minimal clutter and no pets, where a mid-range lidar model with basic obstacle avoidance already handles the actual cleaning load fine and the premium dock's weeks-of-autonomy advantage goes largely unused. The tier earns its price in larger, busier, pet-owning households with complex floor plans where the automation genuinely offsets real weekly maintenance time — not as a default upgrade for a simple home.

Frequently asked questions

Is a premium robot vacuum worth it for a small apartment?

Generally not — the tier's main advantages are automation depth for larger, more complex, or heavily-used homes, and a small apartment with a simple floor plan and light daily mess doesn't generate enough cleaning volume to make weeks-long dock autonomy meaningful. A mid-range lidar model handles a compact one- or two-bedroom apartment's actual cleaning load about as well, at meaningfully lower upfront and consumable cost. The exception is a small apartment with genuinely no floor space for frequent manual bin-emptying or storage for cleaning supplies, where the dock's self-sufficiency has practical value beyond just cleaning performance.

How much floor space does a premium self-empty dock actually need?

More than people expect — premium docks combining a large dust bin, water tank and sometimes a drying unit are noticeably bulkier than a basic charging dock, often requiring a dedicated corner with clearance on multiple sides for the robot to dock reliably. In a small Dublin terraced house or apartment, this can be a genuine constraint worth measuring against actual available space — hallway or utility room corners — before purchase, rather than assuming it will fit wherever the previous, simpler dock sat. Some brands publish dock dimensions on the spec sheet specifically for this reason, worth checking before ruling a space in or out.

Do premium robot vacuums need an active Wi-Fi and app subscription?

Wi-Fi connectivity, yes, essentially always, since multi-floor mapping, obstacle-recognition updates and remote scheduling all depend on it. A paid ongoing subscription is not standard across the category the way it is for some smart-home cameras — most current premium brands include full app functionality with the purchase price, though a few have introduced optional paid tiers for extras like extended cloud map storage or AI feature updates. Checking a specific brand's current policy before buying is worthwhile, since this has shifted model to model and isn't yet a settled industry norm either way.

Does obstacle-avoidance AI work as well in a dim room or at night?

Somewhat less reliably, based on manufacturer specifications and owner reports — most premium obstacle-recognition systems rely at least partly on an onboard camera, which performs better with adequate ambient light than in a dark room or overnight run. Many premium models include a small headlight to compensate, and lidar or infrared depth-sensing continues working regardless of light level for basic navigation, but object-identification accuracy specifically can drop after dark. Scheduling cleans during daylight or well-lit hours, rather than very early morning or late at night, generally gets closer to the manufacturer's best-case obstacle-avoidance performance.

Do premium robot vacuums hold their resale value?

Reasonably, within the first two to three years — premium models from established brands tend to hold a higher proportion of original price on the Irish secondhand market than budget models, since buyers specifically seeking mapping and automation features are willing to pay more for used premium stock than for a new budget unit. Value drops off faster once a brand releases a clear successor generation with materially better obstacle AI or dock capacity, which happens roughly every one to two years at this tier. Keeping original accessories and the box generally helps resale more for this category than for simpler vacuum-only models, since buyers often want to confirm full dock functionality is intact.
