Buying guide: how to choose

Does a bigger litre number mean a bigger air fryer basket?

Not necessarily. A single-drawer machine stated at 9–10 litres and a dual-basket machine stated at 10.4 litres combined — two baskets of 5.2 litres each — sound equivalent on paper but behave very differently: the single large drum takes a whole chicken or a full tray of vegetables in one continuous space, while the split machine's two smaller compartments often won't fit a chicken over roughly 1.6kg in either basket. Basket diameter, not total volume, is what determines whether a whole joint or a full family-sized tray fits, and manufacturers rarely publish it. Check product photos for a chicken, a pizza, or a comparable reference object shown inside the actual basket before trusting the litre number on its own.

How much time does cooking in two rounds actually cost you?

For a household of five needing roughly five generous portions of chips, a machine with genuine single-layer capacity for three to four adult portions forces a second round, adding 15-20 minutes and meaning the first batch sits cooling, and softening, while the second finishes. A properly sized 9L-plus single drum or a well-matched dual-zone combination avoids this by fitting the full family portion in one pass. The practical Irish comparison is a family of five's typical dinner: a full 900g-1kg bag of frozen chips plus a tray of chicken pieces needs close to 9-10 litres of genuine single-layer basket space to cook in one round without stacking, which is roughly where 'family size' claims on smaller machines start failing the real test. That second round isn't just slower, it also means the reheated first batch loses the crispness that's the entire reason for choosing an air fryer over a microwave in the first place, undermining the appliance's main selling point on the nights it matters most.

Energy cost for a family of five: air fryer versus oven, per week

A family cooking a full dinner in the oven most nights, 2.5kW for 50 minutes including preheat, spends about 79 cent in electricity per meal, or roughly €5.50 across seven dinners a week. A large air fryer at 1.8kW for a slightly longer 25-30 minutes needed for a bigger load, no preheat, uses about 0.83 kWh per meal, costing 32 cent — a saving near 47 cent per dinner, or about €3.30 a week, roughly €14 a month. That's smaller in percentage terms than the saving on single-portion cooking, because bigger loads narrow the wattage gap between the two appliances, but it compounds daily for a family cooking dinner every night rather than occasionally, and it comes without sacrificing the one-round capacity a big household actually needs. Scale that saving to a full year of nightly cooking and it lands close to €170, roughly the price difference between a mid-range and a genuinely large-capacity machine, which is one way to think about whether the bigger unit pays for itself.

Multi-cooker features worth having at family size

At 9 litres and up, several machines add a rotisserie spit for whole chicken, dedicated baking pans, and multiple wire racks for layered cooking, and these are worth evaluating on genuine usefulness rather than spec-sheet length. A rotisserie function is worth having specifically if whole chicken is a regular family meal, since it produces noticeably more even results than resting the bird flat in a basket. Extra racks matter less than they sound, since stacking two layers in a single chamber usually means the top layer shields the bottom from the fan, undermining the even-cooking case for buying an air fryer in the first place. A dehydrate setting is a genuine bonus for family snack prep but rarely decides a purchase on its own. Check the rotisserie's maximum bird weight against what your family actually buys — many spits cap out around 1.8-2kg, which rules out a larger Sunday roasting chicken even on a machine otherwise sized for family cooking.

What a 15L multi-cooker gives up compared to two mid-size machines

Single-chamber machines above 12-15 litres market themselves as oven replacements, but heating a much larger volume of air with one fan and element takes measurably longer to reach temperature than the marketing's 'no preheat' claim implies for smaller machines, even if there's technically no separate preheat step. Space furthest from the fan, typically the bottom corners of a tall chamber, runs measurably cooler, so even cooking across a full family-sized load in one of these units depends more on food placement than in a smaller, more uniformly circulated basket. Families choosing between one large multi-cooker and two separate mid-size machines should weigh counter space and cost against this: two 8L machines cooking simultaneously often finish faster and more evenly than one 15L chamber cooking the same total volume. Two smaller machines also give a family the option to run one now and one later rather than committing the entire meal to a single chamber and a single timer.

Frequently asked questions

Will a large air fryer actually fit a whole chicken?

Depends on basket diameter, not the litre figure. A single-drum machine rated 9-10 litres generally fits a whole chicken up to around 1.8-2kg, since the chamber is one continuous space wide enough for the bird's shoulders. A dual-basket machine with the same combined litre rating often won't, because each individual basket is narrower — a chicken that doesn't fit through the opening of a 5.2-litre single drawer won't cook in it regardless of the combined total. If whole chicken is a regular family meal, prioritise single-chamber capacity and check the manufacturer's maximum stated weight for whole poultry specifically, not the general litre figure.

Is a single big drum better than dual-zone for a family of five?

For genuinely large single items, whole chicken, a full tray of roast vegetables, a big drum wins on raw capacity. For the more common family pattern of cooking two different things at once, chips and chicken pieces, say, dual-zone wins because both zones run independently rather than needing everything to share one chamber and one setting. Many large families end up better served by a big dual-zone unit, roughly 7.5-9 litres combined, than either a smaller single drum or a smaller dual-zone, since it covers both the single-large-item case reasonably well and the two-different-dishes case properly.

How many portions does a 9-10 litre air fryer actually hold, not on paper?

Realistically four to five adult portions of a mixed meal, chips plus a protein, in a single layer without stacking, which is the actual test of whether a machine cooks evenly rather than just holding more food. Manufacturer photos sometimes show more food than that fitted in, but usually stacked, which undermines the airflow the whole appliance depends on. If your household regularly needs six or more portions in one pass, plan on either a genuinely larger single-drum unit above 10 litres or splitting the meal across two machines rather than trusting a single unit's top-line litre claim.

Do big air fryers use noticeably more electricity than small ones?

Somewhat, both in wattage and in slightly longer cook times for the larger loads they're built to hold, but the gap is smaller than most people expect. A large 1.8kW machine cooking a big family portion for 25-30 minutes uses around 0.75-0.9 kWh per meal; a small 1.4kW machine cooking a two-person portion for 20 minutes uses about 0.47 kWh. Per portion cooked, the large machine is actually more efficient, since it's spreading a similar preheat-free overhead across more food. The comparison that actually matters isn't large versus small air fryer, it's any air fryer versus the oven it's replacing.

Can a large air fryer completely replace the oven for a big family?

For most weeknight dinners, yes, provided the basket genuinely holds a full family portion in one pass rather than forcing rounds. Where it still struggles is baking that needs oven-specific geometry, a large lasagne dish, a full-size cake, bread — and very large roasting joints beyond what any air fryer chamber accommodates, typically anything over about 2.5-3kg. Most large families that make the switch keep the oven specifically for Sunday roasts, baking and big-batch cooking for gatherings, while putting the air fryer to work the other six nights, which is where most of the weekly electricity saving actually piles up.

Is it worth buying a 15L multi-cooker instead of a dedicated oven?

Not as a full replacement for most families — a 15L chamber heats less evenly than a proper oven's insulated cavity and airflow design, particularly in the corners furthest from the fan, and it can't match an oven's volume for a large gathering or a full baking tray. It's worth buying alongside an existing oven specifically as the daily-use appliance, faster, cheaper per meal, no preheat, while the oven stays for the jobs that need real volume or steady ambient heat. Treating a multi-cooker as the household's only cooking appliance usually means compromising on the meals that need genuine oven capacity.
